As someone who uses AI every day and builds products around it, I've been thinking about a quieter consequence of this technological shift.

The discussion around AI often focuses on what machines are becoming capable of doing. A more important question may be what happens to our own capabilities when we stop practicing them.

Today, many of us use tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and others for tasks that were once routine:

- Performing simple calculations
- Writing emails and messages
- Summarizing information
- Organizing thoughts
- Drafting plans and documents

These are valuable uses of AI. They save time, reduce friction, and help us move faster.

But cognitive skills work much like muscles. When exercised regularly, they become stronger. When used less often, they can gradually weaken.

The deeper challenge is not a competition between humans and artificial intelligence.

It is a question of whether we continue to cultivate the skills that make human judgment valuable: reasoning, writing, critical thinking, and the ability to sit with a problem long enough to understand it.

What makes this moment particularly interesting is how we choose to spend the time AI gives back to us.

In principle, automation should free us to:

- Learn new concepts
- Build more ambitious products
- Explore unfamiliar disciplines
- Reflect more deeply
- Stay current with a rapidly changing world

In practice, some of that reclaimed time is absorbed by notifications, social feeds, and endless scrolling.

This is the paradox of our era.

We have access to tools that can amplify human intelligence at an unprecedented scale, yet that same convenience can reduce the amount of deliberate thinking we do ourselves.

I do not see this as an argument against AI. Quite the opposite.

AI is one of the most powerful technologies we have ever created. Used thoughtfully, it can help us design better systems, discover new ideas, and solve problems that once seemed out of reach.

The responsibility is ours.

We can use AI as a substitute for thinking, or as a catalyst for deeper thinking.

The difference lies in intentionality.

As builders and daily users of these systems, we should continue to ask ourselves:

- Which skills are we delegating?
- Which skills are we strengthening?
- How are we investing the time we save?
- Are these tools making us more capable over the long term?

The future of AI will be shaped not only by the intelligence of our tools, but by the habits of the people who use them.

The most enduring advantage will belong to those who use AI to augment their minds while continuing to exercise the cognitive abilities that define human creativity and judgment.
